The research behind the kits

Every design rule the studio follows comes from a finding. This page lists the finding, links the study by DOI, and says what the kits do with it. Eleven sources, all peer reviewed. Where the evidence is contested, the page says so. Nothing here is a claim the studio has tested on its own; the pilots are where that begins.

Removing the deciding

A card is an if-then plan

Gollwitzer and Sheeran (2006), Implementation Intentions and Goal Achievement: A Meta-analysis of Effects and Processes, Advances in Experimental Social Psychology 38. doi:10.1016/S0065-2601(06)38002-1. Across 94 studies, plans of the form "when situation X arises, I will do Y" raised goal attainment by a medium to large effect, by handing the start of the action to the situation rather than to willpower. Every Hard Day Kit card is written in that form: the hour is the cue, the card is the response.

Choices cost more when they are many

Pignatiello, Martin and Hickman (2020), Decision fatigue: A conceptual analysis, Journal of Health Psychology 25(1). doi:10.1177/1359105318763510. The authors define decision fatigue as impaired decision quality after a sequence of decisions, and note that the laboratory evidence for a depletable resource is contested. The kits do not depend on the mechanism. They depend on the observation the authors hold stable: at the end of a day of deciding, one more decision is where the plan fails, so the card makes it.

Structure outside the head does the work

Barkley (1997), Behavioral inhibition, sustained attention, and executive functions: Constructing a unifying theory of ADHD, Psychological Bulletin 121(1). doi:10.1037/0033-2909.121.1.65. Barkley's model treats ADHD as a disorder of the internal regulation of behavior across time, and his practical conclusion is that accommodation works by putting the regulation outside the person, at the point of performance. A card on the fridge at 5:40 is the point of performance.

Checklists lower the load on experts too

Haynes and colleagues (2009), A Surgical Safety Checklist to Reduce Morbidity and Mortality in a Global Population, New England Journal of Medicine 360. doi:10.1056/NEJMsa0810119. A nineteen-item checklist cut deaths and complications in eight hospitals among people who already knew every item on it. The kits' rules pages and working pages are checklists for people who already know what to do and cannot, at that hour, hold it.

One thing at a time, because working memory is small

Sweller (1988), Cognitive Load During Problem Solving: Effects on Learning, Cognitive Science 12(2). doi:10.1207/s15516709cog1202_4. Sweller's account of working memory as the bottleneck in problem solving is why every kit page holds one task, why the cards show three steps at most, and why the phone page shows one card at a time.

Timing, stopping, and the evening

Time is harder to feel with ADHD

Barkley, Murphy and Bush (2001), Time perception and reproduction in young adults with attention deficit hyperactivity disorder, Neuropsychology 15(3). doi:10.1037/0894-4105.15.3.351. Adults with ADHD reproduced time intervals less accurately than controls, with the gap widening at longer durations. The kits put time outside the body: a sand timer the child turns, a visible timer at bedtime, a card that names the minutes out loud.

The late hour is a known pattern

Kroese, De Ridder, Evers and Adriaanse (2014), Bedtime procrastination: introducing a new area of procrastination, Frontiers in Psychology 5. doi:10.3389/fpsyg.2014.00611. The authors name and measure going to bed later than intended with no external reason, and find it tied to self-regulation and to daytime fatigue. The 11 pm card treats the pattern as expected and gives the one action, the phone in another room, that the authors' account points to.

Light is a lever

Cajochen (2007), Alerting effects of light, Sleep Medicine Reviews 11(6). doi:10.1016/j.smrv.2007.07.009. Light, and short-wavelength light in particular, raises alertness and suppresses melatonin on a scale of minutes. The 7:15 card's instruction to turn off the overhead light and the amber night light in the storeroom follow from it.

Motivation, rewards, and the lapse

Choice sustains effort

Patall, Cooper and Robinson (2008), The effects of choice on intrinsic motivation and related outcomes: A meta-analysis of research findings, Psychological Bulletin 134(2). doi:10.1037/0033-2909.134.2.270. Across 41 studies, giving people choices raised intrinsic motivation, effort, and performance. The child chooses the book and draws the card. The mother draws the card. Nobody is assigned one.

Rewards undermine the thing rewarded

Deci, Koestner and Ryan (1999), A meta-analytic review of experiments examining the effects of extrinsic rewards on intrinsic motivation, Psychological Bulletin 125(6). doi:10.1037/0033-2909.125.6.627. Tangible rewards given for doing an activity reduced later free-choice engagement with it across 128 studies. No kit has a prize, a streak, or a sticker chart. Rank counts sessions and is never taken away.

Kindness after a lapse brings people back

Breines and Chen (2012), Self-Compassion Increases Self-Improvement Motivation, Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin 38(9). doi:10.1177/0146167212445599. Across four experiments, a self-compassionate response to a failure raised motivation to improve relative to self-criticism or self-esteem framing. The lapse card, the fairness clause, and the rule that nobody evaluates a hard-day dinner are the kits' versions of that response.

A typeface for the reader who struggles

Rello and Baeza-Yates (2013), Good fonts for dyslexia, ASSETS 2013. doi:10.1145/2513383.2513447. Sans serif, monospaced, and roman faces improved reading performance for readers with dyslexia in an eye-tracking study. The Agency is set in Lexend throughout for that reason, and every kit's working pages use a sans face at a large size.

Studies were verified by DOI against Crossref in October 2026. Where an abstract summarizes a study, the summary is the studio's and the study's authors did not review it.
