The Hard Day Kit

Founding edition

Or find the hour you are in.

Twenty five cards, written in the first person by the person who made this, grouped by the kind of hour they are. Nothing needs setting up. Every card works tonight with nothing on the fridge. The sheet, the rules, the board, the drafts are things the cards make for you, one good day at a time. The dots are how much capacity a card assumes. Each card is three parts, and the rest folds under "more, if you want it." Nothing here is stored anywhere but on this phone. Add this page to your home screen and open it when it is the hour it is.

What the cards are built on

Every instruction comes from one of these, or is marked on the card as the studio’s own practice. Verified by DOI against Crossref in October 2026. The summaries are the studio’s.

A card is an if-then plan. Gollwitzer and Sheeran (2006), Advances in Experimental Social Psychology 38. doi:10.1016/S0065-2601(06)38002-1. "When X, I will Y" plans raised goal attainment across 94 studies. Every card is one, with the hour as X.
Decisions run out. Pignatiello, Martin and Hickman (2020), Journal of Health Psychology 25(1). doi:10.1177/1359105318763510. Decision fatigue defined, with the lab evidence noted as contested; the cards depend only on the observation that the last decision of the day is where the plan fails.
Structure outside the head. Barkley (1997), Psychological Bulletin 121(1). doi:10.1037/0033-2909.121.1.65. Accommodation for ADHD works at the point of performance: the fridge, the card, the board.
Checklists work for experts too. Haynes et al. (2009), New England Journal of Medicine 360. doi:10.1056/NEJMsa0810119. A nineteen-item checklist cut surgical deaths among people who knew every item. The morning list and the reset are checklists for a person who knows what to do and cannot hold it.
Working memory is small. Sweller (1988), Cognitive Science 12(2). doi:10.1207/s15516709cog1202_4. One task per page, three steps at most, one card at a time.
Time is harder to feel with ADHD. Barkley, Murphy and Bush (2001), Neuropsychology 15(3). doi:10.1037/0894-4105.15.3.351. Visible timers, clock times said aloud, screen-off as a time and not a warning.
The late hour has a name. Kroese, De Ridder, Evers and Adriaanse (2014), Frontiers in Psychology 5. doi:10.3389/fpsyg.2014.00611. Bedtime procrastination measured and tied to self-regulation and daytime fatigue.
Light is a lever. Cajochen (2007), Sleep Medicine Reviews 11(6). doi:10.1016/j.smrv.2007.07.009. Light raises alertness within minutes; the overhead light off and the amber night light follow from it.
Rewards undermine the thing rewarded. Deci, Koestner and Ryan (1999), Psychological Bulletin 125(6). doi:10.1037/0033-2909.125.6.627. No streaks, no prizes, no sticker charts, and no fight over a plate.
Spread the effort. Cepeda, Pashler, Vul, Wixted and Rohrer (2006), Psychological Bulletin 132(3). doi:10.1037/0033-2909.132.3.354. The same hour spread across the week beats one night of it.
Aversiveness drives avoidance. Steel (2007), Psychological Bulletin 133(1). doi:10.1037/0033-2909.133.1.65. The call, the email, the meeting: shrink the task and the delay and the avoidance shrinks with them.
Unstructured social tasks cost the most. Gelbar, Smith and Reichow (2014), Journal of Autism and Developmental Disorders 44. doi:10.1007/s10803-014-2135-5. The scripts exist because the open conversation is the hard part.
Kindness after a lapse brings you back. Breines and Chen (2012), Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin 38(9). doi:10.1177/0146167212445599. The lapse card, the fairness clause, and the one-sorry rule.
Two books, not studies, and said so on the cards: Kennedy, Good Inside (2022), for the shape of the words to say, two things are true and the line still holds; Davis, How to Keep House While Drowning (2022), for the reset. Not in any study, and said so on the cards: the door room, the four-item lunch, the one-base Sunday, the three drafts for a sick day, body doubling at homework, and the sentence for relatives. The studio uses them and will not pretend they are proven. One card is held back. The visible-first fridge, a practice passed around by people with ADHD online and described at dopakitchen.com, is written and sits in the file untested. It goes into the kit when the person who made it has done it in her own kitchen and logged it here.
