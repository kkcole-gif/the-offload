Any hour

For whoever lives with her.

This page is four lines and a paragraph. It is the back of the directions page, and it is for a partner, a co-parent, a grandparent, or a friend who is in the house on the hard days. It is short on purpose, because the hard day is not the day to read.

  • 1Do not ask what you can do. Asking hands her one more decision. Read the Household Sheet on the fridge, pick the thing you own, and do it. Say what you took.
  • 2Draw a card yourself. Rule 5 is for you. A house where one person runs the system is a house where that person is the system.
  • 3Do not evaluate a hard-day dinner. Rule 2. Cereal is dinner. The commentary costs more than the meal.
  • 4The Fairness Clause binds you. She wrote it on a good day for a bad one. On the bad one, it is the rule and you are not the exception.

Why it is built this way

Executive function, the set of skills that start, stop, sequence, and decide, runs out before competence does, and it runs out fastest at the end of a day of deciding. Accommodation works by moving the structure outside the person at the point where it is needed, which is why the rules are on the fridge and the cards are on the phone rather than in anyone's head. A plan written as "when this hour comes, do this" gets carried out far more often than an intention does. A kind response to a lapse brings people back, and a critical one does not. The studies, linked by DOI, are on the research page.

If you are a therapist or a coach

The kit is a set of implementation intentions on a phone, a checklist on a fridge, and a self-authored rule for hard days. It makes no clinical claim and is not a treatment. If a client of yours uses it, the Tally page shows which hours she reaches for, which may be useful to you, and the Fairness Clause is a document she wrote about what she needs, in her own words.
